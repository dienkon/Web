Supplementary Note 2. Transition-dipole analysis of the excited state
The excited-state transition dipole analysis was performed using the VASP WAVECAR obtained
from the constrained excited-state calculation at the relaxed excited-state geometry. The relevant hole
and electron states were identified from orbital-resolved electronic projections. To locate the dominant
contributions to the localized excitation, we evaluated the simultaneous Cr-d localization of the
occupied and unoccupied states for all k-points and selected those exhibiting the largest combined
localization on the excited Cr ion.
The transition dipole matrix elements were subsequently evaluated using the Python package
VaspBandUnfolding and its vaspwfc.py module,[1, 2] which reconstruct pseudo-wavefunctions directly
from the VASP WAVECAR file. The calculations were performed for the spin-orbit-coupled
noncollinear electronic structure using the corresponding spinor wavefunctions. The dipole matrix
elements were obtained through the momentum-matrix formalism implemented in vaspwfc.py, which
evaluates the pseudo-wavefunction matrix elements between the selected initial and final states and
converts them to dipole-like transition amplitudes through the standard momentum-position relation.
For each selected k-point, the transition dipole vector d=(d ,d ,d ) was evaluated between the

occupied and unoccupied excited-state orbitals. The polarization anisotropy was quantified through the
corresponding dipole intensities |d |2. In the employed lattice convention, the Cartesian x-direction

To visualize the spatial anisotropy of the excitation, charge-density redistribution plots were
constructed from partial charge densities corresponding to the excited-state LUMO and ground-state
HOMO evaluated at the relaxed excited-state geometry. The resulting real-space charge redistribution
was analyzed in the crystallographic ab-plane.
